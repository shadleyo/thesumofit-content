Here's what the government's own forecasters expect. Before the war, oil closed at seventy-two dollars a barrel. The Energy Information Administration now expects it to average a hundred and five for the rest of this year.

At the pump, gas was two ninety-eight before the war. Today it's four twenty-six. The forecast: an average of three fifty-six next year. Lower, but still well above where it started.

Diesel is over six dollars a gallon, and is forecast to drift to about four fifty next year, compared with three seventy-six before the war. And homes that heat with oil are expected to pay about twenty-one percent more this winter.

Even the forecast that brings prices down never brings them all the way back. That's the sum of it.
