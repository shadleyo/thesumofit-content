Seven months of war with Iran. And the average American household has already paid about two thousand dollars for it. Here's where it went.

Start at the pump. Before the war, a gallon of regular cost two ninety-eight. Within weeks it passed four dollars. Today, it's four thirty-three. Diesel, the fuel that moves almost everything you buy, hit a record six fifty-three in September.

Gas is up forty-five percent. Diesel, sixty-eight. Jet fuel has nearly doubled, and airfares are up twenty-two percent on a year ago.

Moody's Analytics adds it up. Eleven hundred dollars in energy. Five hundred and forty in higher interest rates. Three hundred and sixty for the military campaign itself. And the energy bill grows by about fifty dollars every week.

Now the part that's hard to believe. Americans have paid one hundred and sixteen billion dollars extra for gas and diesel. The Pentagon's official cost for the war is forty-three point six billion. At the pump, Americans have paid more than double.

The war is fought far away. The bill comes home. That's the sum of it.
