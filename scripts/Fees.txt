A two percent fee sounds tiny. Over thirty years, it isn't.

Meet Alex and Sam. Both invest three hundred dollars a month for thirty years. Same markets, same seven percent return. The only difference is the fee. Alex pays a quarter of a percent a year. Sam pays two percent.

For the first ten years, you'd barely notice the gap. But compounding works on fees too, and the gap keeps widening.

After thirty years, Alex has three hundred and forty-eight thousand dollars. Sam has two hundred and forty-nine thousand.

That's almost a hundred thousand dollars, lost to a fee that sounded tiny. Because fees don't just take their percentage. They take the growth that money would have earned.

Check the fees on every fund you own. Small fees. Big money. That's the sum of it.
